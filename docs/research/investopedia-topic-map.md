# Investopedia topic map and gap analysis

**Date:** 24 October 2026
**Purpose:** Check that Bullpen's planned course covers what a serious learner meets on Investopedia, and say what to add.

**How this was built.** Investopedia blocks automated access. Both the search tool and the page fetcher refused investopedia.com in this session, so no Investopedia page was read and nothing here is taken from its text. The map uses (a) Investopedia's known top-level areas (Investing, Trading, Markets, Economy, Personal Finance, Dictionary, Academy courses), as described by third parties, and (b) the standard concept set those areas teach. Each area was checked against reputable educators (CFA Institute, SEC investor.gov, FINRA, CME Group, the Options Industry Council, StockCharts ChartSchool, CFI, Morningstar, Brookings). Concept names are standard terms that belong to nobody. Worth a 15-minute spot-check in a normal browser before treating this as an exact Investopedia list.

**Key:** Built = in Units 1-6 today. Planned = in the stage plan (planned units have no lesson lists yet, so coverage is judged from their titles).

---

## 1. Topic map (concept names only)

**1. Investing basics**
- Saving vs investing, goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, emergency fund first
- Asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash, real assets), risk and return, diversification
- Compounding, rule of 72, fees, inflation's effect on cash
- Lump sum vs regular investing (dollar-cost averaging), buy-and-hold vs trading, active vs passive

**2. Stocks**
- Shares, ownership, common vs preferred, share classes and voting
- Exchanges, tickers, market hours, indices (S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq) and how they are weighted
- Market cap and size bands (large, mid, small, penny), shares outstanding, float
- EPS, dividends, blue chips, ADRs and foreign listings, 52-week range, volume

**3. Fundamental analysis and ratios**
- Valuation: P/E (trailing, forward), earnings yield, PEG, P/B, P/S, EV/EBITDA, EV/sales, P/FCF, CAPE
- Income: dividend yield, payout ratio, dividend cover
- Profitability: gross, operating and net margin, ROE, ROA, ROIC, DuPont breakdown
- Liquidity: current ratio, quick ratio. Leverage: debt to equity, net debt/EBITDA, interest cover
- Efficiency: asset turnover, inventory turnover, receivable days, cash conversion cycle
- Qualitative: moat, management, industry structure (five forces), competitive position

**4. Financial statements**
- Income statement: revenue, COGS, gross profit, opex, EBIT, EBITDA, interest, tax, net income, basic vs diluted EPS
- Balance sheet: current and long-term assets and liabilities, equity, retained earnings, goodwill, working capital, book value
- Cash flow: operating, investing, financing, capex, free cash flow
- How the three link, accrual vs cash, annual and quarterly reports (10-K, 10-Q), MD&A, footnotes
- Adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings, one-offs, earnings quality, auditor's opinion, red flags

**5. Valuation**
- Intrinsic vs market value, margin of safety
- DCF: free cash flow forecast, discount rate, terminal value (growth vs exit multiple), sensitivity
- WACC, cost of equity (CAPM, beta), cost of debt
- Relative: comparable companies, precedent deals; dividend discount model; sum of the parts
- Equity value vs enterprise value (the net-debt bridge)

**6. Technical analysis**
- Chart types (line, bar, candlestick), timeframes, Dow theory, trends and trendlines
- Support and resistance, breakouts, ranges, chart patterns (head and shoulders, double tops, triangles, flags)
- Moving averages (simple, exponential), golden and death crosses
- Momentum: RSI, stochastics, MACD. Volatility: Bollinger Bands, ATR
- Volume, on-balance volume, VWAP; market breadth (advance-decline)
- Candlestick patterns (doji, hammer, engulfing); Fibonacci, Elliott wave
- Backtesting, the efficient-market critique

**7. Options and derivatives**
- Basics: call, put, strike, premium, expiry, contract size, ITM/ATM/OTM, American vs European, exercise, assignment
- Pricing: intrinsic and time value, Black-Scholes inputs, implied vs historical volatility, IV rank, skew and smile, put-call parity, VIX
- Greeks: delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho
- Strategies: long calls and puts, covered call, protective put, collar, cash-secured put, vertical spreads (debit and credit), straddle, strangle, butterfly, iron condor, calendar, diagonal, rolling
- Risks: assignment, early exercise, pin risk, IV crush, wide spreads, leverage
- Futures: contract specs, notional value, margin, marking to market, settlement, hedgers vs speculators, contango and backwardation, roll; forwards, swaps, perpetual futures

**8. Bonds and fixed income**
- Face value, coupon, maturity, zero-coupon bonds, T-bills, notes and bonds
- Price and yield move opposite, current yield, yield to maturity, yield to call
- Duration, convexity, interest-rate risk, reinvestment risk
- Credit ratings, investment grade vs high yield, credit spreads, default and recovery
- Government, municipal, corporate, inflation-linked, callable; sovereign debt and Eurobonds
- Yield curve shapes, bond ladders, bond funds

**9. ETFs and mutual funds**
- Mutual funds, NAV, ETFs, index funds, closed-end funds, money market funds, target-date funds
- Expense ratio, loads, AUM, tracking error, bid-ask spread and liquidity
- Creation and redemption, authorised participants, premium and discount to NAV
- Sector, bond, commodity, international, thematic, factor (smart beta), ESG funds
- Leveraged and inverse ETFs and daily-reset drag

**10. Portfolio management and risk**
- Asset allocation (strategic vs tactical), rebalancing, correlation, concentration
- Modern portfolio theory, efficient frontier, CAPM, alpha, benchmarks
- Standard deviation (volatility), beta, Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, maximum drawdown, value at risk
- Position sizing, stop-losses, home bias, currency risk

**11. Trading mechanics**
- Brokers, market makers, bid-ask spread, liquidity, slippage
- Market, limit, stop, stop-limit and trailing-stop orders; time in force (day, GTC, IOC, FOK)
- Settlement (T+1), pre-market and after-hours, halts and circuit breakers
- Margin accounts, initial and maintenance margin, margin calls
- Short selling, borrow fees, short interest, short squeezes
- Day, swing and position trading; trading journal

**12. Behavioural finance**
- Loss aversion, prospect theory, disposition effect
- Overconfidence, herding and FOMO, anchoring, confirmation bias, recency bias
- Mental accounting, sunk cost, gambler's fallacy, hindsight and availability bias
- Bubbles and manias, efficient markets vs behavioural view

**13. Macroeconomics for investors**
- GDP, business cycle, recession, leading vs lagging indicators, PMI
- Inflation (CPI), deflation, stagflation, real vs nominal returns and rates
- Central banks, policy rate, open market operations, QE and QT; fiscal policy
- Jobs data, consumer confidence, yield curve and inversion
- Exchange rates, devaluation, money supply, sovereign default

**14. Alternative assets**
- Crypto: Bitcoin, Ethereum, blockchain, wallets and custody, exchanges, stablecoins, DeFi, halving, volatility
- Commodities: gold, oil, farm goods, spot vs futures prices, inflation hedges
- Real estate and REITs; private equity, venture capital, hedge funds; collectibles

**15. Time value of money**
- Simple vs compound interest, compounding frequency, future and present value, discount rate
- Annuities, perpetuities, NPV, IRR, CAGR, opportunity cost

**16. Corporate actions**
- Cash and stock dividends; declaration, ex-dividend, record and payment dates; DRIPs
- Stock splits, reverse splits, buybacks, secondary offerings and dilution, rights issues, share-based pay
- IPOs (underwriters, prospectus, pricing, lock-ups), direct listings, SPACs
- Mergers and acquisitions (premium, tender offer, merger arbitrage, hostile bids), spin-offs, delisting, bankruptcy

Also large on Investopedia but out of scope for us: budgeting, credit cards, mortgages, insurance, US retirement accounts, tax, broker reviews and "best stocks" lists.

**Ordering problems found**
- Bonds (Foundations) and DCF need present value, but TVM sits in the Vault. Put a short present-value lesson into Bonds and interest rates, or move TVM into Foundations.
- EV/EBITDA is planned, but enterprise value is never taught. Give it its own lesson first.
- The season ranks players on risk-adjusted return, yet risk measures arrive last, in Stage 4. Players need them before Season 1 (Feb 2027).

## 3. Recommended additions (most important first)

### 3.1 Measuring risk (fills the planned Risk management unit; schedule before Season 1)
**Stage:** Trading and risk, or move to the end of Foundations. Why: the game scores on risk-adjusted return, so players must understand what is being measured.
- **Lessons:** Volatility and standard deviation; Drawdowns and the maths of recovery; Beta and correlation; Sharpe ratio: return per unit of risk; Position sizing and stop-losses.

### 3.2 Reading price trends (technical analysis basics), new unit
**Stage:** Trading and risk. Confirmed. It is one of Investopedia's largest areas, every trading app shows these indicators, and players will see them on day one. Teach what each tool measures and be honest that the evidence for prediction is mixed. Leave out Elliott wave, Fibonacci and pattern catalogues: weak evidence, and they read as tips.
- **Lessons:** Trends and trendlines; Support and resistance; Moving averages; Momentum: RSI and MACD; Volume and the limits of charts.

### 3.3 Money, rates and the economy (macro and central banks), new unit
**Stage:** Analysing stocks, before Sectors and the economy. Confirmed. Rates and inflation move every asset in the game, and the lira's collapse and dollarisation make this concrete for Lebanese students (accurate history only). Keep the bond-maths side in Bonds and interest rates. This unit covers who sets rates and why.
- **Lessons:** Inflation and real returns; Central banks and interest rates; The yield curve; Economic data that moves markets; Currencies and exchange rates.

### 3.4 ETFs in depth, new unit
**Stage:** Foundations, after Building a portfolio. Confirmed. ETFs are tradable in the game. The Index funds lesson covers only the basic idea, and leveraged ETFs are a classic beginner trap.
- **Lessons:** How an ETF works (NAV, creation and redemption); ETFs vs mutual funds; Reading an ETF (costs, holdings, tracking, spread); Kinds of ETF (sector, bond, commodity, factor, thematic); Leveraged and inverse ETFs.

### 3.5 Company events (corporate actions, IPOs, earnings), new unit
**Stage:** Analysing stocks. Confirmed. These events make held stocks jump or drop during a season (ex-dividend drops, earnings gaps, takeover premiums). Splits are already in the Market cap lesson. Mention reverse splits and spin-offs in passing.
- **Lessons:** Earnings season: beats, misses and guidance; Dividends: the dates that matter; Buybacks and dilution; IPOs and going public; Mergers and takeovers.

### 3.6 Futures basics, new unit
**Stage:** Vault. Confirmed, but low priority. Futures are not tradable in the game. Still, they explain gold and oil prices and the roll drag in commodity ETFs. They also cover crypto perpetuals, where young traders really do get liquidated.
- **Lessons:** What a futures contract is; Margin and marking to market; Hedgers and speculators; Contango, backwardation and roll costs; Crypto perpetuals and liquidation.

### 3.7 Health-check ratios, new unit
**Stage:** Vault, next to Returns ratios. It completes the ratio families that Investopedia teaches and that Reading company accounts only touches.
- **Lessons:** Liquidity: can it pay its bills?; Leverage and interest cover; Turnover and efficiency; Working capital and the cash cycle; Quality of earnings (adjusted figures, one-offs).

### 3.8 Greeks and volatility in depth, new unit
**Stage:** Vault, after Advanced option strategies. For the keenest options learners only.
- **Lessons:** Gamma; Vega and implied volatility; Rho, dividends and early exercise; Put-call parity; Volatility skew and the VIX.

### 3.9 Lesson lists for planned units, so the gaps close inside them
- **Building a portfolio:** Goals, horizon and risk tolerance; Asset allocation; Correlation; Rebalancing; Lump sum vs regular investing.
- **Bonds and interest rates:** What a bond is; Present value and bond prices; Yield to maturity; Duration; Credit ratings and default (Lebanon's 2020 Eurobond default is accurate local history).
- **Valuation ratios:** P/E in depth (trailing, forward, earnings yield); PEG; P/B and P/S; Enterprise value and EV/EBITDA; Dividend yield and payout. Do not repeat the basic P/E lesson.
- **Growth, value and dividends:** add factor investing (size, value, momentum, quality) and one card on ESG.
- **Sectors and the economy:** Sectors; Cyclical vs defensive; The business cycle; Sector rotation; Commodity-linked sectors.
- **Advanced option strategies:** 6 planned topics for 5 lessons. Suggested: Straddles and strangles; Iron condors (with credit spreads); Calendar spreads; Earnings and IV crush; Rolling and assignment. Put collars and cash-secured puts in an Option strategies replay lesson or in 3.8.
- **Short selling and margin:** Buying on margin; Margin calls; How short selling works; Short squeezes; Liquidity, spreads and slippage.
- **Market psychology:** Loss aversion and the disposition effect; Overconfidence; Herding, FOMO and bubbles; Anchoring and confirmation bias; Recency bias and mental accounting.
- **Crypto and other assets:** Bitcoin and blockchains; Stablecoins, exchanges and custody; Gold and commodities; Real estate and REITs; Scams and red flags.
- **Time value of money (Vault):** Present value; Discount rates; Annuities and perpetuities; NPV; IRR and CAGR.
- **Valuing a company (Vault):** Intrinsic vs market value; Forecasting free cash flow; WACC and CAPM; Terminal value and sensitivity; Comparables and margin of safety.

### 3.10 Rejected, with reasons
- **Personal finance** (budgets, credit cards, mortgages, insurance, US retirement accounts): large on Investopedia, but US-centric and not our product.
- **Tax:** country-specific. One generic card at most.
- **Forex and CFD trading, day-trading rules:** high-risk retail products and US rules. Currencies are covered as macro (3.3).
- **Advanced chart methods** (Elliott wave, Fibonacci, Ichimoku, candle-pattern catalogues): weak evidence, and they would read as trading tips.
- **Exotic derivatives** (swaps, CDOs, CDS): beyond student investors. One card at most, in a 2008 history lesson.
- **Hedge funds, private equity, VC in depth:** students cannot access them. One card in Crypto and other assets is enough.
